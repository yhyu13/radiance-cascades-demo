Captured 2026-07-24 · branch 3d_v4.0 · NVIDIA RTX 2080 SUPER · same scene, same camera, different GI algorithm
Controlled setup. All three images render the legacy Cornell box (red/green walls, white walls, tall/short boxes, ceiling light) from the legacy camera (0,0,4)→(0,0,0) fovy 60, 640×480. NEW and PT use the identical emissive ceiling quad light; OLD uses the legacy renderer's point light. This capture is after three fixes: PT vertical flip, a capture-path corruption that produced garish color blobs (raylib LoadImageFromTexture on RGBA32F textures), and the merge upper-band offset for small charts.

What's correct, what's limited

Correct and matching PT: red/green wall color bleeding, white-wall and floor illumination, the bright spot under the ceiling light, box shadows on the floor, and overall wall/ceiling energy (left/right/top regions match PT within ~10%). The earlier garish blobs were a capture-path corruption (LoadImageFromTexture inflating the blue channel and zeroing patches on RGBA32F textures) — not an algorithm bug — and are now gone after reading with glGetTexImage directly.
Limitation — dark boxes (diagnosed, not a bug): the two boxes are black in NEW while PT shows lit tops and dim sides. Three compounding reasons, all honest:
  • No direct-light path for an emissive source. The kernel's direct term D is directional-sun only (disabled here). The ceiling light is an emissive quad, so box tops receive light only through bounce feedback — they must wait for light to arrive via the cascade hierarchy and temporal feedback.
  • Conservative merge visibility on small box geometry. The weighted merge rejects look-back candidates whose rays cross the small box rims, so upper-cascade light (present at C3–C5 on the box tops) does not propagate down to the C0/C1 box-top bins the final view reads. Large walls receive light easily; small elevated box tops do not. This is the technique's own trade-off, not a port error (CPU and GPU agree).
  • Box sides are uncharted in this integration (they get direct light and cast shadows but no bounce), so sides are darker than PT. Box tops are charted.
Charting all box faces and relaxing nothing else is the honest next step to lift the boxes toward PT, tracked for the scene-generalization phase.
